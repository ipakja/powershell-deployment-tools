Examples

How an engagement with BIT can run

The following examples are illustrative sample cases, not client references. They show how BIT structures clearly scoped user, access and workplace tasks — from approval through to documented close-out.

Sample: employee departure

Starting point

Someone is leaving the company. It is unclear which accounts, mailboxes and access rights remain active and who may approve deactivation.

Required details

  • Leave date
  • Known systems and accounts
  • Person authorised to approve
  • Whether devices or shared mailboxes are involved

Process

  • 1Capture starting point and affected systems
  • 2Obtain approval for deactivation
  • 3Handle approved changes within the agreed scope or coordinate technical execution with the responsible specialist function.
  • 4Document close-out plus open points and risks

Required approvals

Written approval from the responsible manager before any access change.

Outcome

Documented overview of handled access, confirmation of deactivation steps and a list of open points.

Sample: new employee starts on Monday

Starting point

A new employee should start ready to work. Required access, licences and devices are not yet clearly assembled.

Required details

  • Start date and role
  • Required systems (e.g. Microsoft 365)
  • Device needs if known
  • Who may approve access

Process

  • 1Clarify needs and approvals
  • 2Arrange setup of approved accounts, rights and licences and document them.
  • 3Check readiness on day one
  • 4Record the outcome in writing

Required approvals

Approval for accounts, rights and any licences before setup.

Outcome

Checklist of set-up access, known limitations and next steps if anything is missing.

Sample: Outlook does not work at a workstation

Starting point

Outlook is unusable at a workstation. It is unclear whether only this workstation or further users are affected, and whether the cause sits with the account, the device or the service.

Required details

  • Affected user and workstation
  • Exact error message or observed behaviour
  • Since when the issue has existed
  • Whether further people are affected
  • Recent changes to account, device or software

Process

  • 1Narrow whether account, device or service is affected
  • 2Handle within the confirmed scope or coordinate with the responsible specialist function
  • 3Follow the work through
  • 4Document the outcome and open points

Required approvals

Access to the affected workstation only by agreement with the responsible person.

Outcome

Documented root-cause narrowing, measures taken or coordinated, and a list of open points.

Sample report structure

How an anonymised close-out can look — without client names and without specially protected personal data.

Starting point

Short description of the need and the agreed scope.

Checked points

Which systems, accounts or workplaces were reviewed.

Finding

What was observed — factual, without exaggeration.

Changes made

What was implemented or coordinated within the agreed scope.

Open points and risks

Which points are still open, which dependencies exist and where additional specialist expertise is required.

Next steps

Recommended follow-ups with clear priority.
